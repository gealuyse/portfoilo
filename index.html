Around the table

Shared understanding is the only way to build anything significant.

Design doesn't happen in a vacuum. It happens in meetings, Slack threads, and rough prototypes — I spend as much time writing and explaining as I do on pixels. If the engineer, the PM, and the designer aren't seeing the same problem, they won't build the same solution.

About

Natthapath Damrongsri — I work at the intersection of complex logic and everyday use.

Enterprise SaaS, a mobile app used by over a million people, and operational tools where a confusing interface has a real cost. Complexity never really disappears — in good design it just stops being the user's problem. My job is to absorb it while designing, so the screen stays quiet.

I stay quiet at the start too. I'd rather understand the messy reality before proposing a clean solution. And I care about what gets left behind — clean files, clear docs, a system that keeps working after I've moved on.

  • Start from what's there.
  • Make a first useful version. (Perfection is for iteration three.)
  • Reduce confusion before adding polish.
  • Respect what can't change; question what can.
  • Don't make people relearn.
  • Learn enough for the next decision.
