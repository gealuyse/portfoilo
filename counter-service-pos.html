Counter Service POS — three levers, zero moves

Cashiers process hundreds of transactions per shift on muscle memory — the positions their hands already know. The screen had to get clearer without making any of that wrong. Only three levers were available: spacing, grouping, and contrast. This page pulls them in front of you.

Plate 01 · The constraint

Everything was locked except spacing, hierarchy, and contrast.

The flow was fixed. The buttons were fixed. The brand was fixed — and cashiers had muscle memory built around all of it. Any change that forces relearning costs the operator real money in slower transactions. The asymmetry of this table is the brief:

Plate 01 — Locked vs. adjustablethe inventory that shaped every decision

Locked — do not touch

  • Button positions — every control stays where hands know it
  • Flow order — the transaction sequence is trained behavior
  • Control sizes — targets tuned for speed over years
  • Brand — colors and identity fixed by the operator
  • Muscle memory — the invisible system underneath all four

Adjustable — the three levers

  • Spacing — breathing room inside and between zones
  • Grouping — transaction areas separated from action areas
  • Contrast — weight where the eye should land first

Five things locked, three levers free. The design question became: how much legibility can three levers buy without spending a single pixel of position?

TradeoffThe result is less dramatic than a full redesign. That was the point — a cashier should not have to relearn the screen to read it better. Familiarity is a feature; disrupt it at real cost.
Plate 03 · The hardware

Build layout rules for two screen realities.

The same system had to hold on legacy 800×600 terminals and 1920×1080 displays — both live in the field. Two layout profiles, one set of rules: the compact profile prioritizes essential metadata; the wide profile gains room without simply enlarging everything.

What I'd do differently

The entire design rests on one claim: better hierarchy and spacing speed up checkout without moving a single control. A claim that strong needs evidence, not confidence.

The test I'd run

If it reaches live testing, log keystroke timing on the terminals — real transaction data against the old layout, not estimates.
