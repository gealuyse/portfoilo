Counter Service POS A redesign that had to feel like no change at all

I revised the hierarchy of Counter Service POS screens under a strict constraint: control positions and transaction flow had to remain familiar. The work used three levers — spacing, grouping, and contrast — across 800×600 and 1920×1080 layouts.

Challenge

Cashiers run this screen on muscle memory. Everything that would normally be redesigned — positions, flow, sizes, brand — was locked.

Approach

I preserved control positions and used spacing, grouping, and contrast to separate transaction areas, distinguish risk, and make the primary action easier to identify.

Plate 01 · The constraint

Everything was locked except spacing, hierarchy, and contrast.

The flow, controls, and brand were fixed, and cashiers already knew the screen through repetition. Moving familiar controls would introduce relearning into a time-sensitive workflow. The brief therefore separated what could not change from the three properties still available:

Plate 01 — Locked vs. adjustablethe inventory that shaped every decision

Locked — do not touch

  • Button positions — every control stays where hands know it
  • Flow order — the transaction sequence is trained behavior
  • Control sizes — retained from the existing interface
  • Brand — colors and identity fixed by the operator
  • Muscle memory — the reason for preserving the first four

Adjustable — the three levers

  • Spacing — breathing room inside and between zones
  • Grouping — transaction areas separated from action areas
  • Contrast — weight where the eye should land first

Five things locked, three levers free. The design question became: how much legibility can three levers buy without spending a single pixel of position?

Plate 03 · The hardware

Build layout rules for two screen realities.

The specification had to account for both 800×600 terminals and 1920×1080 displays. Two layout profiles used the same rules: the compact profile prioritized essential metadata, while the wide profile gained room without simply enlarging everything.

What I'd do differently

The proposal assumes clearer grouping helps cashiers identify the next action without disrupting familiar control positions. That assumption was not tested in live operations.

The test I'd run

If tested, compare task completion time, errors, and hesitation across representative transactions on both screen sizes.
